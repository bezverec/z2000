# z2000 0.2.0

z2000 is a JPEG 2000 Part 1 codec and JP2 converter written from scratch in
Zig. 0.2.0 is its first release: it decodes every T.803 profile-0 and
profile-1 conformance stream, converts TIFF, BMP, PNG, baseline JPEG,
LinearRaw DNG, and normalized-linear OpenEXR input to lossless or lossy JP2,
writes JP2 back out as TIFF, and on one thread encodes as fast as Kakadu
8.4.1 and decodes faster. Unsupported input fails closed rather than
producing output whose behaviour is not implemented.

This release is the code of `0.2.0-rc.7` with one error-reporting change
(below); the seven release candidates
since July 2026 are summarised here, and their notes and
`docs/changelog.md` have the details.

## What It Does

- **Decode** every JPEG 2000 Part 1 codestream in its checked envelope:
  reversible 5/3 and irreversible 9/7 at every Part 1 precision (1 to 38
  bits), any component count and sampling, single and multi-tile layouts
  with tile-local COD/COC/QCD/QCC overrides, all five progression orders and
  POC, PLT/PLM/TLM/PPM/PPT, every code-block style (BYPASS, RESET, TERMALL,
  ERTERM, vertically causal, segmentation symbols), RGN Maxshift ROI, quality
  layers, resolution reduction, and region and tile selection. All 16
  official ITU-T T.803 profile-0 streams and all 7 profile-1 streams decode
  against their references.
- **Encode** lossless (RCT, 5/3) and lossy (ICT, 9/7, scalar quantization,
  global PCRD rate allocation) JP2 with quality layers, every progression
  order and tile-part division, POC, PLT/TLM/PPM/PPT, SOP/EPH, and the
  code-block styles above; `zraw-to-j2k` encodes any Part 1 component
  layout (up to 16384 components of 1 to 38 bits, signed or unsigned, with
  their own sampling) reversibly, with Maxshift ROI.
- **Convert** TIFF 6.0 (strips or tiles; uncompressed, LZW, Deflate,
  PackBits, or JPEG; 1 to 16 bits; RGB, gray, alpha; ICC), Windows BMP, PNG,
  baseline JPEG (with Exif/XMP/IPTC preserved into JP2 UUID boxes),
  LinearRaw DNG, and normalized-linear OpenEXR to JP2 (`z2000`/`z2k`, with
  extension-inferred commands and wildcard batches), and JP2 to TIFF.
- **Interoperate**: Kakadu 8.4.1, OpenJPEG 2.5.4, and Grok 20.4.19 decode
  z2000's output, and z2000 decodes theirs, across the style, multi-tile,
  rate, and adapter interop scripts in `tools/`.

## Performance

On an Intel Core i5-14500 (2048x2048 RGB, 8 bits; `docs/benchmarks.md`, the
0.2.0-rc.6 release checkpoint):

| Operation | Threads | z2000 | Kakadu 8.4.1 | OpenJPEG 2.5.4 | Grok 20.4.19 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| lossless encode | 20 | 82 ms | 70 ms | 125 ms | 137 ms |
| lossless encode | 1 | 469-483 ms | 506-507 ms | 745-752 ms | 761-787 ms |
| lossless decode | 20 | 74 ms | 68 ms | 106 ms | 117 ms |
| lossless decode | 1 | 454-486 ms | 581-595 ms | 640-649 ms | 598-617 ms |

From rc.4 to this release (the candidates' interleaved comparisons on the
same files), the lossless encode went from 138 to 83 ms at 20 threads and
from 1037 to 485 ms at one, and the lossless decode from 124 to 73 ms and
from 812 to 468 ms; every speed-up kept the codestreams and the decoded
samples byte for byte identical.

## Since 0.2.0-rc.7

- An HTJ2K (Part 15) codestream is now refused as `UnsupportedPayload`
  instead of `InvalidCodestream`: it is valid, and HTJ2K support is planned
  for 0.3.0.

## In 0.2.0-rc.7

- **Fixed:** a resolution-reduced decode (`--reduce`, or
  `resolution_reduction` in the API) on more than one thread failed with
  `InvalidCodestream` on most images taller than a few hundred rows; rc.5
  and rc.6 are affected, one thread was not. The block coverage check gave
  a band of rows below the reduced image a reversed range. Reduced decodes
  now give the same samples on any thread count.
- On more than three threads the lossless encoder transforms only the first
  5/3 level before Tier-1 and codes that level's detail blocks while the
  coarser levels are still being transformed (about 1 ms at 20 threads).
  Codestreams are byte-identical to rc.6's.

## Release Process

- Every archive was built and tested on a local machine, as for the release
  candidates: native Windows gates, the Linux x86-64 and RISC-V 64
  ReleaseFast suites in a Linux container (RISC-V under QEMU), and the Part 1
  corpus with the official T.803 streams (`tools/release_local.ps1`).
- **No macOS archive** is published: hosted Actions minutes, which the native
  macOS build needs, are exhausted, and a cross-built binary could not be
  tested here. macOS users can build from source with Zig 0.17.

## Stability And Scope

The CLI and Zig API remain unstable before 1.0. This is not a general JP2/JPX,
camera RAW, or HDR converter. Each input adapter has a deliberately narrow
documented envelope; old-style or progressive JPEG, CCITT, LZMA, and ZSTD
TIFF compression, Adam7 PNG, CFA/demosaicing, compressed or tiled EXR,
general ICC transforms, extended XMP, and semantic metadata rewriting remain
unsupported. JP2 metadata payloads are preserved but are not yet restored into
TIFF output.

The project's 100/100 ISO engineering scorecards are internal coverage tools,
not formal ISO conformance certification; passing the T.803 streams is
evidence, not certification. The binaries are unsigned; verify the platform
archive against `SHA256SUMS`. Every archive contains identical `z2000` and
`z2k` executables for the long and short command names.
